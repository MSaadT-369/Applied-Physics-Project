Understanding Refraction

Refraction is the bending of light as it passes from one medium to another due to a change in its speed. The refractive index determines how much the light will bend; a higher refractive index indicates light will slow down more.

Applications

Refraction plays a crucial role in optics. It's essential in the functioning of lenses that magnify images or focus light in cameras and other optical devices.

Total Internal Reflection

When light travels from a denser medium (like water) to a rarer medium (like air) at a steep angle, it can reflect back into the denser medium instead of refracting. This phenomenon is known as total internal reflection.

Critical Angle

Total internal reflection occurs when the angle of incidence exceeds a certain critical angle, given by:

\( \theta_c = \sin^{-1} \left( \frac{n_2}{n_1} \right) \)

Where \( n_1 \) is the index of refraction of the denser medium and \( n_2 \) is the index of refraction of the rarer medium.
